# PrepBun Customer Persona

Oct 2, 2026 · @Jason

## Persona snapshot

PrepBun's customer is a self-driven high school student chasing a top-tier SAT score, backed by parents who want the best program available, not the cheapest. She already works hard; she wants a tutor good enough to get her the last points.

> "I already know the basics. I need someone who can find exactly what's holding me back and fix it, fast." (Composite quote, written from the research)

|  | Maya, the Self-Starter |
| --- | --- |
| Role | High school junior preparing for the SAT |
| Starting point | Already scoring well, or disappointed by a first score "by my standards" |
| Target | A near-perfect SAT score and admission to an Ivy League or similarly elite school |
| Drives the purchase | Maya finds and pushes for the program; her parents fund it |
| Wants | Precise, personalized, data-backed instruction from someone who has mastered the test |
| Success looks like | Hitting her target score and getting into her first-choice school |

## Who they are

Maya is the one doing the work and the one who sought out help. In the research, motivated students reached out to tutors themselves, while less motivated students came through their parents.

- **She's already capable.** One student started at 1460 and wanted help only on "niche areas" and "tough problems."
- **She respects expertise.** She wants a tutor who understands her strengths and weaknesses without being told.
- **She values her time.** She looks for prep that's "time-efficient and nuance-based," not a course that repeats what she knows.

### The parents behind her

Maya's parents are drawn to prestige and want the highest-value program for their kid, regardless of cost. They see prep as an investment in an elite admission, not an expense to minimize. They're reassured by credentials, data and selectivity, and they like that their child had to qualify.

## Who we're for and who we filter out

PrepBun works with self-starters who want mastery, and its messaging should make everyone else self-select out before they apply.

|  | Who we're for | Who we filter out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivation | Drives her own prep; reaches out herself | Needs a tutor or parent to make her do the work |
| What she wants | Real mastery of the math and reading behind the test | Quick tricks and shortcuts only |
| Target | A top score for a top school | "Good enough" to clear a minimum |
| Test | The SAT | The ACT |
| How the family sees cost | An investment in the highest-value option | A price to shop around on |

Messaging should name these standards openly. Saying who PrepBun isn't for makes the right families want in.

## Goals and motivations

Maya's goal is almost always a specific school, and the SAT score is how she gets in. She wants that score as fast as possible, on a schedule that fits her life.

- **A specific school.** An Ivy League school, a dream university, a competitive program like pre-law. The score is the ticket, not the goal.
- **Get there fast.** She wants her target score soon. She doesn't yet know that tricks alone can't get her there, and that fixing her foundations is the fastest route.
- **Prep that fits her life.** She's busy with classes and activities, so she needs one-on-one help she can book anytime, at her own pace.
- **A top score, not a passing one.** She's aiming for the highest score she can reach.
- **Quiet prestige.** She and her parents respond to a program that's selective, the way her target colleges are.

## Pain points and objections

Maya's frustration is a plateau: she's strong, but generic prep can't find the specific gaps keeping her from a top score.

- **No time to spare.** Between classes, sports and activities, fixed lesson slots don't fit, and late or no-show fees punish a busy week.
- **Rationing help.** Hour packages make her save sessions just when she needs more.
- **Generic programs waste her time.** One student found big-name courses less useful than prep that was "heavily personalized."
- **Plateaus she can't explain.** Tricks lift a middle score quickly, then stall. The last points need real command of the math and reading, which she may not realize until she's stuck.
- **Hidden math gaps.** Weak math foundations hold back the SAT math score and rarely show up until the hardest questions.
- **Practice tests feel too easy.** One person had heard practice tests are "way easier" than the real thing, so she wonders whether a practice score will hold.
- **Parents' worry: is it actually the best?** Cost isn't the issue; picking a program that isn't strong enough is.

## How they decide

Maya and her parents choose the program that looks hardest to get into and most likely to work. They judge on four things.

- **Who the tutors are.** Reviews in the research name individual tutors, so the person matters. A tutor with a perfect SAT score is proof of mastery she can't argue with.
- **Data and results.** Score gains and where students got in carry more weight than promises.
- **Selectivity.** Applying and being accepted feels familiar, like the college process she's already in, and signals she's joining something exclusive.
- **Risk.** A money-back guarantee tied to her target score removes the last doubt for parents who want certainty.

Timing clusters around junior year, with motivated students often prepping over spring and summer.

## What PrepBun offers them

PrepBun is SAT-only, with unlimited access to tutors who all scored a perfect 1600. Every program fixes foundations first and teaches test strategy second, and progress is tracked with data.

| Program | What it is | Pricing | How to get in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unlimited SAT tutoring | Unlimited sessions with a perfect-score tutor | Month to month | Application |
| Unlimited math + SAT math | Unlimited tutoring in school math and SAT math, for students whose math foundations need work | Month to month | Application |
| Score guarantee program | Tutoring toward a set target score, with a full refund if the student doesn't reach it | Set per student and goals | Application and interview |

Why SAT only: PrepBun sees the SAT as more precise and more math-driven than the ACT, which suits students who want mastery. Tutors are hired on two things only: a perfect SAT score and real skill at teaching. Degrees and accolades don't count.

## Messaging guidance

**Core message:** Get the SAT score your dream school is looking for, with one-on-one tutoring that fits your schedule.

Talk about Maya first: her school, her schedule, her score. PrepBun's standards are the quiet proof underneath, not the headline. Every message should answer one of her questions: *Will this get me into my school? Will it fit my life? How fast?*

| Pillar | What Maya hears | Quiet proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your school | We start with where you want to go and work back to the score | Students' stories, naming the schools they got into |
| Your schedule | Book anytime, as often as you need, with no late or no-show fees | Unlimited, month-to-month sessions |
| The fastest real route | We find exactly what's costing you points and fix it | Diagnostics, section-level gains |
| Tutors who've done it | Your tutor has already reached the score you're aiming for | Every tutor scored a 1600, mentioned once and plainly |
| A program worth applying to | We look for students with perfect-score potential | The application itself |
| Peace of mind (parents) | Reach the target score or get a full refund | Guarantee program terms |

### Tone

Warm, assured and understated, like a great college counselor: confident because of results, never boastful. Say "you" and "your school" more than "we." Mention credentials once and let them speak. Selectivity is an invitation ("see if you're a fit"), never a warning.

### Words to use and avoid

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| your school, your dream school, the Ivy League | "elite," "the best," superlatives about PrepBun |
| anytime, on your schedule, unlimited | packages, hours, fees |
| apply, see if you're a fit | "we don't take everyone," "not for you" as headlines |
| your target score, faster, what's costing you points | hacks or tricks, whether selling them or knocking them |
| tutors who scored a 1600 (once) | stacked credentials, "world-class" |
| SAT | ACT, "SAT/ACT" |

### The hacks question

Recommendation: keep "no hacks" out of the marketing. Arguing against tricks sounds like a lecture to someone who just wants a higher score fast. Instead, meet her desire for speed and reframe it: the fastest way to her score is finding what's actually costing her points and fixing it, with strategy layered on top. Save the full foundations-first explanation for the application and interview, where it also screens out students who only want shortcuts.

### Let the application do the filtering

The application signals the bar without anyone having to say it. Ask for her target school, current score and goals, so the right students lean in and the wrong ones self-select out. A line like "We work with students aiming for top scores" sets the standard without sounding exclusive for its own sake.
